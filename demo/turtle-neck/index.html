Turtle-Neck Alert

Watches for forward head posture (“turtle neck”) with nothing but a laptop's front webcam. It first learns two postures from you, sitting comfortably and pushing your head forward, then alerts you when your neck stays near the second one for 10 seconds. It compares you only with yourself, so it is a reminder, not a diagnosis.

Everything runs in your browser with MediaPipe. The video never leaves your device; only your calibration is saved, in this browser.

How it works
  • Neck model. Pose Landmarker finds the shoulders; Face Landmarker fits a 3D face mesh and head pose. In camera coordinates the neck runs from the shoulder midpoint to the head center. Head depth comes from the face fit, torso depth from shoulder width scaled off your calibration. The indicator is how far that neck has tipped toward the screen, in degrees.
  • Leaning in. Leaning in with the whole upper body moves head and shoulders together, so the angle barely changes; pushing only the head forward raises it.
  • Pauses. No reading while the face or either shoulder is out of view, or while the head is turned more than 20° or tilted more than 10° from your calibration.
  • Alert. Median of the last 15 readings, scaled so 0 is your relaxed posture and 1 your calibrated forward posture. Above 0.5 for 10 s gives a beep and a notification, at most once a minute.
  • Limits. Assumes your head sits above your shoulders when you calibrate, so calibrating while already slouched makes the slouch your baseline. Rounded shoulders also read as head forward. Browsers slow down or pause background tabs, so keep this page visible, for example in a small window.

Prototype; not a medical device.
