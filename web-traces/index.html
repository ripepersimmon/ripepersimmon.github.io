Your AI, On a Dial: Agent Retrieval Experiment

Traces from the agent experiment in “Your AI, On a Dial” (arXiv:2608.22852). Three open-weight models (Qwen3-8B, Mistral-Small-24B, Gemma4-12B-it) run a three-turn research loop over a live news search engine: they write four search queries, pick four of the ten returned results to open, then read them and write a memo ending in a buy/sell decision. Pick a company and two arms to compare the traces side by side.

Setup
  • Search. Bing News (past month) via ddgs. Results are snapshot-cached, so identical queries return identical pages in every arm.
  • Arms. Baseline; the dial (an additive shift on one MLP neuron); a decision-token logit offset calibrated to the same decision index π = (buy − sell) / (buy + sell); contrastive activation addition (CAA); a second neuron from the same model; write-norm-matched placebo neurons; and, for Qwen3-8B, the dial applied on turn 1 only, on turns 2–3 only, or with result pages frozen to the baseline's.
  • Labels. Each sentence of headline and snippet is labelled favourable, unfavourable or neutral for an investor in the company, by an un-intervened Qwen3-8B judge or by Gemini.
  • Sample. 150 held-out S&P 500 companies × 2 trials (buy/sell option order swapped), greedy decoding.
  • Result page. Shown in the order the agent saw it, fused rank in parentheses. “Padding” marks an item from a fixed neutral seed query rather than from the agent's own queries.

Research artefact; not investment advice.
